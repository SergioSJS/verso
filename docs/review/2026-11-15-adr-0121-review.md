## Summary

**7 BLOCKING, 12 IMPORTANT, 9 MINOR.** All seven BLOCKING findings are
visibility leaks that the text, accepted as recommended, would build in. None
needs a different architecture: each is a missing rule or a rule stated too
loosely, and each fix is a sentence or an `[OPEN]` in the ADR.

What holds: the storage keeps a board a valid JSON Canvas (fields under
`verso`, known node types; O4 to be re-observed, as the ADR says); the ADR
states the right principle (*"a player is never served what is not theirs,
on every path"*) and picks the stricter option where it sees the choice
(Q5 server-side sight, Q6 tiles withheld); no code or dependency of Atlas is
proposed; the roadmap row says "Proposed, nothing decided" and promotes
nothing.

What does not hold: the principle is not carried to the details. The
projection that exists today (`board_sight::shape_canvas_seen`) filters only
`nodes`, `edges` and `verso.drawing`; every new top-level key (`walls`,
`fog`, `grid`) passes through it untouched unless the ADR says otherwise, and
the ADR does not. The "wholly under fog" rule and the "tiles wholly under fog"
rule both leak by construction. And the sentence that T1–T6 carry *"no
secrecy risk beyond T4's"* is wrong for T3, T5 and T6.

## BLOCKING

### B1. Top-level `verso.walls` and `verso.fog` reach a player in the file's bytes

> 7. … per wall `blocks` (`sight+light`, `sight`, `light`, `move`); doors with
> `open`, `locked`, `secret` …
>
> 8. … **painted fog in the board** (`verso.fog`, ordered paint/erase shapes …)

**Why.** Checked in the code: `shape_canvas_seen` (`board_sight.rs:385`)
clones the canvas and filters `nodes`, `edges` and `/verso/drawing` only.
Every other key under the top-level `verso` is served as it is — to a player
who sees the board, to a player's synced device, and through `lay_over`.
`verso.walls` is served whole today (0120 writes it; a Watabou door carries
`"kind": "secret"`). With 0121 as written, a player's copy of the file holds:
every wall of the unexplored map (the whole floor plan), every secret door
with the word `secret`, every locked door, and the fog's ordered paint/erase
list — which says more than the fog's present outline (an erased stroke is
what the game master revealed and covered again; a "room-from-walls" shape is
a room's outline). Item 10 says *"nothing unseen is sent"*, but only for
vision (T9); T6 writes walls three steps earlier and T8 writes fog.

**Fix.** One rule in item 1, valid from the first step that writes any of
them: *"a top-level `verso` key of this layer is never served to anyone but
the game master on any path; the projection is an allowlist of top-level keys,
not a denylist"* — then say per key what a player is given instead (`grid`:
whole; `walls`: nothing, see B6 for T7; `fog`: only the present covered area
as one merged outline, never the list). Add to Consequences that
`shape_canvas_seen` changes to an allowlist in the same step as T6, with the
canary test. Record in the PR that `verso.walls` with secret-door kinds is
served today (0120): a fact for the maintainer, not decided here.

### B2. "Wholly under fog" leaks everything that is only partly under it

> An element whose box lies wholly under fog is **Gone** for a player

**Why.** An element partly under fog is served whole. A drawn corridor that
starts in a revealed room and runs into the dark, a text card across the
fog's edge, an arrow pointing at a hidden room, a region outline: all reach
the player complete. It is worst where walls come from: a Watabou dungeon is
*Verso's own drawing* (0120 item 2), not a picture, so item 9's tiles protect
nothing there, and any drawn element larger than one room is never "wholly"
under a room's fog. The bounding box is also the wrong test for a diagonal
line (its box is far larger than the line). And the rule as written makes the
**map's own picture card** Gone after "cover all", while item 9 serves it as
tiles: the two items disagree on the same element.

**Fix.** Make it an `[OPEN]` with the safe recommendation: an element is
served to a player only if it lies **wholly outside** the fog (touching fog =
Gone), except the map picture (item 9's rule) and, if wanted, elements the
game master marks "always shown". Say that an imported dungeon's drawing must
be cut by room for fog to work on it (or that fog on a drawn dungeon is not
offered until it is), and that an edge is Gone when either end is.

### B3. Item 9 (b) leaks the map through coarse tiles and through tiles the fog only touches

> tiles wholly under fog are not served

**Why.** The tiles are a pyramid: *"each level above it is half the one
below, until one tile holds it"* (`maptiles.rs`). The top levels are one or a
few tiles covering the whole map; they are never wholly under fog unless the
whole map is. A player asking for the top level is served the entire map at
low resolution — enough to read a dungeon's layout. At every level, a tile the
fog covers in part is served with the art under the fog in it (256 px of
leak around every fog edge at level 0, 4,096 map pixels at level 4). The ADR
calls (a) *"a visibility leak by Verso's rule"*; (b) as written is the same
leak at lower resolution.

**Fix.** (b) must say **the pixels under fog are blanked on the server**
before a tile is served to a player (a tile wholly under fog is then not
served, a tile partly under it is served masked), at every level; the masked
tile is made per request or cached per fog state, and its cost is measured on
the 1 GB server (I7). The same for the "sight" of T9.

### B4. The picture file "hidden while it is a fogged map": the other paths are not named

> the picture file itself is hidden from players while it is a fogged map

**Why.** A new access rule (a file's visibility derived from the state of a
board that shows it) stated in one clause, with these paths open: (1) the
same picture as a card on **another board**, or embedded in a note, or the
picture's **copy**; (2) a player's **device that already synced the
picture** before the fog was painted, the browser's cache of the file and of
unmasked tiles, the phone's offline copy — nothing served can be taken back;
(3) the picture's and the board's **version history** from before the fog
(0091); (4) `board_preview` (a projected command today, `reader.rs:55`),
hover preview (0085), "show to the table" (0110 item 9), the board exported
as a picture (0090 item 6 asks only about secret pins); (5) share links
(`share_board.rs`) — the ADR speaks only of "a player", but a board is also
read by a reader of a share (0081, 0087), who has no table. It also sits
outside `may()`: 0113's point is *one* answer, and here a second source of
"does not see" appears that the Access dialog does not show.

**Fix.** Turn the clause into an `[OPEN]` with its sub-cases written out, as
0113 K did: recommended, that painting fog on a map **asks the game master to
set the picture to "Does not see"** through the one model (so `may()` stays
the only answer and the dialog shows it), warns when the picture was already
served or synced to someone ("already on Ana's device"), and that the tiles
are served by the board's right, masked (B3). State that fog applies to
**every non-owner reader** — table players, share readers, link readers — and
list the paths in item 8 again with the ones missing: share links, device
sync, `board_preview`, hover preview, show to the table, export by anyone,
thumbnails, the outline/panel lists, the browser's cache. State the
irreversible part plainly: covering again does not take back what was served.

### B5. A token is a file card of a note the players do not see: either the statblock is released or the token is a grey box — and the bar, the picture and the numbers leak

> **a file card for a note** (a character, a creature, a typed note) … drawn
> round with the note's picture … the HP (or any) tracker of the note's
> statblock as a bar … Several tokens may name one note (goblin 1, 2, 3
> numbered) … Hidden token = `secret` (exists).

**Why.** By 0113 H, a card of a file the player does not see is **Locked**:
*"never the file's path, its name, its colour, its text"*. A monster's note
is exactly such a file. So a visible monster token is one of two things, and
the ADR chooses neither: (a) a locked marker — no picture, no name: not a
token; or (b) drawn with the note's picture, name and HP bar — which serves a
player parts of a note they do not see (name, image property, tracker value),
a new exception to 0110 item 18 that nobody decided. Three more leaks ride on
it: the **HP bar** of a creature (exact hit points; Atlas shows names and HP
*"only if switched on"*); the **numbering** (players seeing goblin 1 and
goblin 3 know a hidden goblin 2 exists; the survey notes Atlas counts *"only
what players see"*, the ADR dropped that); and a Locked marker still tells
*"its place and its size"* — acceptable for a hidden handout card, not for a
creature the players should not know is in the room unless the game master
remembers to mark it `secret` too.

**Fix.** Q2 must carry the question: *what does a player see of a token whose
note they do not see?* Recommended: the token node carries its **own** public
face in the board (`verso.token.name`, `verso.token.image` naming a picture
file whose access is its own, both optional and set by the game master), the
note stays hidden, and a player is served only that face; the bar is served
to a player only for notes they see, or as a game-master switch per token
(off by default) that sends a coarse state, never the number; numbering is
per viewer or stored per token, never a count over hidden ones; a token of a
hidden note with no public face is Gone, not Locked. Remove *"no secrecy risk
beyond T4's"* from T3.

### B6. Presence is not filtered by fog or sight: the drag, the ruler and the pointer say where hidden things are

> A player's drag is a presence; the drop is the edit …
>
> 3. **Ruler.** … shared live as a presence (as the laser is), never written

**Why.** Today a player's presence is an allowlist (`user`, `pointer`,
`button`, `selected` cut to what it sees — 0113 S7 review, point 2). 0121 adds
two presences (a token's drag, a ruler) and two reasons an element is unseen
(fog, sight) and says nothing of presence beyond *"presence selection"* in
item 8. As written: the game master dragging a `secret` or fogged token
streams its path to every player; a ruler measured from a hidden token draws
its start point; with Q7 "each player's own tokens", Ana's window receives
the live drag of Bruno's token that her token cannot see; the game master's
pointer and laser inside fog show where they are working. Also the wall
check: *"Walls block a player's token on a move (server check)"* — a refused
drop tells the player a wall is there, in fogged or unexplored space too, and
a movement-only wall (`move`) is invisible by definition: a player can map
walls by probing drops, which is what 0113 S7's *"No activity oracle"* was
written against.

**Fix.** In items 3, 5 and 10: a drag or a ruler is relayed to an account
only while **both ends and the dragged element** are in what that account
sees (the element's class and its fog/sight), decided by the server in
`presence::for_player`; the game master's pointer and laser are cut inside
fog for players (or an explicit "hide my pointer while preparing" default);
a canary test for presence under fog. For the wall check, record the oracle
as an `[OPEN]`: recommended, a move is judged against walls only along a path
the account's sight or explored area covers, and refused outright into fog
(one answer, "you cannot see there"), so a refusal never says more than the
picture does.

### B7. Initiative names what is hidden, and T5 comes before the rules that would hide it

> the turn change written to the log; players see the order without secret
> tokens (the server filters)
>
> T1–T6 are the small wins (each useful alone, no secrecy risk beyond T4's …)

**Why.** Only `secret` tokens are filtered. Not filtered: a combatant whose
**note** the players do not see (its name is the hidden file's name — B5); a
combatant under fog or out of sight (T8/T9 come after T5 and the ADR does not
say T8 and T9 revisit the tracker); *"notes with no token"* (no board element
to carry `secret` at all). The **log** is a second path: a turn change
"written to the log" for a hidden creature, and its initiative roll through
the dice of 0088, land in a log every member reads unless the entry has an
audience (0107 secret rolls). The round counter is a small oracle too (a
round that takes one more "next" than the players can count).

**Fix.** Item 12: a tracker entry has its own **shown to players** state,
default **hidden** for anything that is not a player's own character; the
name a player sees is the token's public face (B5), never the note's; rolls
of hidden entries are secret rolls (0107); a turn change of a hidden entry is
not written to the players' log and is shown as nothing (or, as an option of
the game master, as "a hidden creature acts"); T8 and T9 each list "the
tracker follows fog/sight" as part of the step. Correct the sentence about
T1–T6.

## IMPORTANT

### I1. Choices written as statements under "nothing here is decided" (rule zero)

> **Proposed (2026-11-15).** Nothing here is decided; every real choice is an
> `[OPEN]` with a recommendation.

**Why.** The Status lists Q1–Q11. Accepting them "as recommended" (the usual
answer) would also accept, unasked: the "wholly under fog" rule (B2); the
wall kinds and the `secret` door (item 7); the ruler as presence, never
written (item 3); sight **on drop** (item 10 — it is inside Q5's
recommendation but is a separate choice); the whole design of initiative
(item 12; only *where it lives* is a question); the player window (item 13,
no question); the grid's fields, diagonals rules and numbering (item 2); the
fog's tools; and item 15's "Not taken now" list — a decision to leave out
levels, auras, encounters and area templates that is the maintainer's.
*"Hidden token = `secret` (exists)"* reads as a fact; it is the choice B5
questions.

**Fix.** Either add questions (Q12 what a player sees of a token, Q13 what
"under fog" means for an element, Q14 the ruler/drag presence rule, Q15 what
of initiative players see, Q16 the player window, Q17 the not-taken list) or
write in Status: *"accepting Q1–Q11 accepts no other sentence; items 2, 3, 7,
12, 13, 15 are the author's design, `[PROPOSED]`, each confirmed in the PR of
its step"* (as 0110 did for phase 2).

### I2. "No owner field … one access model" is a change to 0113's table, not a use of it

> Recommended: **the account that may edit the token's note** … no owner field
> and no new store, one access model. On a board with a grid, a player moves
> only their tokens (and draws only if the board allows)

**Why.** Two changes to 0113 B / K.6, neither named as one. (1) A player with
only *Sees* on the board but *Edits* on their sheet **writes the board**
(moves a node): a board write without *Edits* on the board. (2) A player with
*Edits* on a board that has a grid can **no longer** move what *Edits* lets
them move today (K.6: *"what Edits covers"*); *"draws only if the board
allows"* is a new per-board switch outside the Access dialog. `may()` has no
action for either. Also unsaid: who moves a token whose note two players
edit; whether a co-owner's phone (capped role) counts as the game master.

**Fix.** Q3 states it as an amendment to 0113 section B: a new action
(`MoveToken`) with its row in the one table, answered by `may()`, shown in
the Access dialog; and says what *Edits* on a gridded board means. Mark the
contradiction with K.6 for the maintainer rather than resolving it in a
recommendation.

### I3. A player's own token entering fog; unseen ids; "explored" serving stale or live positions

> the player's shadow receives only the elements in sight or explored

**Why.** (1) A player drops their token under fog: by item 8 it becomes Gone
for them, and by H an edit to an id not seen is refused — the player has
lost their own token. (2) *"in sight or explored"*: explored memory is for
the map. A token standing in an explored but currently unseen area would be
served at its **live** position — the monster that moved behind the party.
(3) What explored memory *is* (a mask, polygons, per cell) is not said, nor
its bound; Atlas's PNG mask is named in the table only. (4) "Hide again" /
"Forget explored" cannot take back what a device holds (B4).

**Fix.** Say: a player's own tokens are always Open to them; a move into fog
is refused (B6). Explored areas serve **the map and what the game master
marks static**, never tokens or anything that changed since last seen; an
`[OPEN]` for last-seen ghosts. An `[OPEN]` for the explored state's form,
recommended per grid cell (bounded by the grid, cheap on the 1 GB server).

### I4. Readers in a live shadow reverses two accepted decisions; T2 and T3 lean on it before T7

> 6. **Readers follow the board live.** `[OPEN] Q8`. … Recommended: a
> read-only shadow room for them

**Why.** Correctly an `[OPEN]`, but it should say it amends 0090 (revised
2026-10-15, pick 2: *"a reader is refused a board's room and follows the
saved file"*) and 0113 H (*"A player who only sees a board is served the
projection, as today"*), and whether it covers share readers or only table
players. Order: T2's ruler *"shared live"* and T3's tokens reach nobody who
only *Sees* the board until T7; before T7 a player with *Edits* moves every
token and one with *Sees* moves none. T2 and T3 are useful alone for the game
master and a second window only; the table says "Needs T1".

**Fix.** Name the two amended sentences in Q8. Split T7: **T7a** readers in a
read-only shadow (needs Q8; no secrecy change, the shadow exists) placed
before T2/T3 are called "shared", **T7b** players move only their tokens.
Say in T2/T3 what works before T7a.

### I5. The order: the wall editor is in T9 but T7 and T8 need walls; T10 needs T8; T5's dependency

**Why.** T7 *"walls block moves"* and T8 *"room-from-walls"* have walls only
from a Watabou or Universal VTT import until T9 brings the editor. The editor
has no dependency on vision. T10 *"Needs 0110 item 13 (V.43)"* but a player
window without fog projection shows the TV everything the ADR hides; it also
needs the projection to run **without a server** (see I6). T5 says "—" but
its secrecy needs B5's public face (T3) or its own.

**Fix.** Move "wall and door editor" to its own step before T7b (S–M), keep
T9 as vision + explored. T10 "Needs T8 (and T9 if vision is on), V.43". T5
"Needs the entry's shown/hidden state (B7)". Recheck the sizes after the
BLOCKING fixes: T3, T5 and T8 each grow.

### I6. A local folder with no server: where fog, sight, explored state and initiative live

> **what was explored per table in the server's database** … Q9: recommended
> state in the server's database per table

**Why.** A game master at one table with a TV (T10, the most common Atlas
use) or playing solo has a local folder and no UniVerso; the app is a server
only when turned into one (0117). `board_sight` lives in `verso-server`, not
`verso-core`; item 10 puts sight in `verso-core` but not the projection.
"Per table" also leaves out a board in a folder that is not a campaign
(0110: a table is a campaign folder). For 0012: state in a database is
defensible as session state (the log's precedent), but then backup, moving
the folder to another server and the app-as-server's own database
(`VERSO_DATA_DIR`) hold the only copy of a campaign's exploration — say so,
and say "Add to note"/export exists for explored state as for initiative.

**Fix.** Add to Q4/Q9: where the state lives for a local folder
(recommended: the sidecar/config outside the tree per 0011, same shape), and
that the projection (`see`/`shape_canvas`) moves to `verso-core` so the
desktop's player window shows the same thing the server serves, through the
contract (invariant 1). Say what a board outside a table gets.

### I7. Phone and the 1 GB server: JPEG/WebP tiling contradicts an accepted pick; per-move work is unbounded

> tiling then covers JPEG and WebP too (0090 tiles PNG only)

**Why.** 0090's revision of 2026-10-15 accepted *"only a PNG is cut into
tiles"* because a PNG streams by row and *"a JPEG or a WebP is decoded whole
by every decoder"* (`maptiles.rs`): a 16,000-pixel JPEG decoded is about
1 GB, the server's whole memory. (b) needs it, so (b) either breaks the 1 GB
bound or forbids fog on large non-PNG maps; the ADR notices neither. Per-move
cost is also unpriced: with vision, every drop recomputes sight for each
player, changes each player's projection, and so the **projected file's
revision** (`revision_of`) and what every synced device of every player is
told — a file change per token move for N players, plus masked tiles (B3).
`verso.fog` as an ordered list of brush strokes grows without bound inside
the board file, and each stroke is a file write and a history version (0091).
Consequences says "bounds … measured"; no figure is proposed and no step
carries the measurement.

**Fix.** Q6 says: fog on a map needs a PNG (or a JPEG/WebP under a stated
pixel bound, converted once by the game master's device, never on the
server). Propose bounds as numbers for the maintainer (walls, fog shapes,
tokens, sight computations per second per account through `pace.rs`), fog
compacted to its merged outline on save, device sync of a fogged board
debounced, and a measuring task on VM 107 and on the maintainer's phone as
the **first** item of T8 and T9, as 0090 item 4 did.

### I8. Fog and walls are in board coordinates; the map can move without them

> anchored to the map's group so moving the map moves them

**Why.** Said for walls only. Fog (`verso.fog`) and the grid's `origin` are
top-level too. If the map picture moves or is resized — in Verso, or **in
Obsidian**, which keeps `verso` keys but knows nothing of them — the fog
stays where it was and uncovers the map. The same for a board edited by an
outside program or a merge that drops the key: the secrecy of the map rests
on a field other tools may lose.

**Fix.** Store fog, walls and grid **relative to the map node** (its id and
its box), and fail closed: if the map's box no longer matches what the fog
was painted on, or a fogged map has lost its fog key while the server's
record says it was fogged, a player is served the map fully covered and the
game master is told. Add an Obsidian observation to the step (move the map
in Obsidian, reopen in Verso).

### I9. Universal VTT import: the format and its limits are not described

> **Universal VTT** (`.dd2vtt`, `.uvtt`, `.df2vtt`; `[OPEN] Q11`, recommended;
> the format's terms checked first)

**Why.** The terms check is rightly first. Missing: these files embed the
picture as base64 inside the JSON (tens of megabytes) — it must be written
out as a picture file (0063: a picture is a file card, never data in the
board), the parse needs a size bound (0083), and on a phone or the web the
import runs where memory is small; the files carry lights, which Q10 does not
plan — say they are dropped, with a line telling the user; portals map to
doors; the file is an **export of another product**, kept beside the note as
0120 keeps Watabou's (or not). T6 "Needs T1" but it also needs B1's allowlist,
since it is the first step to write secret-bearing walls in bulk.

**Fix.** A short description under item 7 (what is read, what is dropped,
bounds, where the picture goes), "Needs: T1, Q11, the top-level allowlist
(B1)", and the format's source and terms recorded in the ADR when checked,
with `[OPEN]` if no terms are published.

### I10. Atlas VTT (AGPL-3.0-only): source files were read; the ADR should fence the build

> of the source, only English UI strings and the doc comments of a few type
> and protocol files were read, to learn *how a feature behaves*
> (survey, "Licence")

**Why.** 0090 says *"its ideas may be studied, its code is not copied"*; the
survey honours that and is honest about what was opened. But 14 source files
of types and protocol were read by the author of a design whose storage
follows the same shape (ordered paint/erase fog operations; walls with
"blocks" kinds; a per-token vision switch/range/angle). Ideas are free; the
risk is the next step — an implementer opening those files "to see how", or
field names and structures drifting toward Atlas's. The rule for Obsidian
(invariant 13) names admissible sources; there is none written for Atlas.

**Fix.** A line in the ADR's Decision, for the maintainer to confirm: *"The
build never opens Atlas VTT's repository; the survey is the only bridge;
names and structures are Verso's own (0120's `walls` format, the board's
vocabulary). Whether reading its doc comments for the survey was admissible
is recorded here for the maintainer."* Also: any geometry library for sight
or collision is a new dependency (CLAUDE.md §4) — recommended none, pure code
in `verso-core`.

### I11. Accessibility: the player's side and the phone's gestures are missing

> 14. … tokens listed in the side panel as the pins are … moved by the arrow
> keys one cell at a time; the turn announced politely; fog and sight with a
> text equivalent for the game master

**Why.** Missing: (1) the **player's** text equivalent — what is in sight, as
a list made from what the server served (and only that: the panel list and
the board's outline are read paths, B4); (2) a hex has six neighbours and
the arrows four — the key mapping; (3) fog painting and wall drawing without
a pointer (by cells, by room) for the game master; (4) the HP bar's text
value and non-colour state; fog vs explored vs seen not told by colour
alone; grid contrast over any map (the 3:1 floor of 0109 as reference);
(5) the tracker as a list with the current turn exposed (`aria-current`),
operable by keyboard; (6) reduced motion for token glide and camera follow;
(7) on a phone (0076; the maintainer tests there): drag-a-token vs pan vs
paint-fog on one finger, a token's touch target at low zoom, the tracker and
fog tools reachable at 390×844 with a way back — stated as a wish (*"every
control reachable"*), no design.

**Fix.** Extend item 14 with these as requirements per step (T1: hex keys,
grid contrast; T3: target size, bar text; T5: tracker semantics; T8: fog
without a pointer, the player's "in sight" list, one-finger mode switch).

### I12. Paths the "every path" list leaves out

> on every path (file bytes, room, history 0091, export 0090 item 6, search,
> backlinks, presence selection)

**Why.** Beyond B4's: the **index and search** of a board's text cards and
file cards under fog (a card's text is indexed; the list says "search" but

## Verdict

**Not ready for acceptance as written; ready after one revision.** The
direction is sound and consistent with 0057/0090 (a map is a board, fields
under `verso`, Obsidian keeps opening it) and with the server-side rule of
0110/0113. But "accept all recommendations" today would decide seven leaks:
top-level keys served whole (B1), the wholly-under-fog rule (B2), coarse and
partly covered tiles (B3), the picture's other paths (B4), tokens of hidden
notes with their bars and numbers (B5), presence and the wall oracle (B6),
and initiative with its log (B7). Each is fixed by text, not by a new
design. The revision should also turn the unasked choices into questions
(I1), name the amendments to 0113 and 0090 (I2, I4), reorder the steps (I5),
and put numbers and a measuring task on the phone and the 1 GB server (I7).

Contradictions recorded, not resolved here: Q3 against 0113 K.6 (what
*Edits* on a board covers); Q8 against 0090's revision of 2026-10-15 and
0113 H; Q6 (b) against 0090's "only a PNG is cut into tiles". A fact found
in passing for the maintainer: `verso.walls`, with Watabou's secret-door
kinds, is served whole to players today (0120 as built; the drawing shows a
secret door as a dashed line to everyone as well).
